## The question

{/* DRAFT */}
Can explicit maths pick the next book you'll like, and show its working?

## What I built

{/* DRAFT */}
A book tracker that recommends the next read. You rate books with half-stars and sliders; each book has a 12-axis tone profile; the app builds a taste vector from your ratings. It runs entirely in the browser, with no server.

## How it works

{/* DRAFT */}
Predictions use k-nearest neighbours over your own rated books, with a ± band on each one, cosine-similarity evidence for why, and a plain-English reason. A discovery slider runs from comfort shelf to terra incognita. The recommender is unit-tested.

## What it found

{/* DRAFT */}
The maths worked; the inputs didn't. Only 20 calibration books had hand-authored profiles. Automated classification produced absurd or flat profiles, and every fix made the pool safer but smaller.

## Where it breaks

{/* DRAFT */}
The real fix is many users correcting profiles, which crowd platforms have and a solo app doesn't. Similarity also captures what a book is about, not how it is paced.
