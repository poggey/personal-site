## The question

{/* DRAFT */}
Can a Shariah-compliant portfolio match a conventional one on risk? It grew out of my week at Raymond James.

## What I built

{/* DRAFT */}
Shariah-compliant model portfolios matched to Raymond James risk levels 4 to 7, built from UCITS instruments, and a research workbook covering 56+ indices, investable products, screening standards and structural risks, including AAOIFI Standard No. 62.

## How it works

{/* DRAFT */}
Each model is matched on risk first, then on exposure, with sukuk in place of conventional bonds. I built BlackRock Portfolio 360 upload files for the models and compared them against the equivalent conventional ones.

## What it found

{/* DRAFT */}
Risk-matching holds at lower risk (7 bps apart at Risk 4.5) and breaks down at higher risk (108 bps apart at Risk 7).

## Where it breaks

{/* DRAFT */}
At higher risk there are no financials, limited alternatives and a heavy reliance on sukuk. Much of the sukuk available is unhedged USD while the risk targets are in GBP.
