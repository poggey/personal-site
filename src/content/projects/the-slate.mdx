## The question

{/* DRAFT */}
Is A24 a film studio or a venture fund? I treated its filmography as an investment portfolio and tested three hypotheses: returns are skewed, acclaim does not pay, and genre and format explain more than quality.

## What I built

{/* DRAFT */}
A cost and revenue model for 118 films, with a core sample of 58. Data came from the TMDB and OMDb APIs and was cleaned by hand in Jupyter, flagging problem rows rather than dropping them. P&A spend and rental rates are modelled, in real 2025 dollars, with every assumption recorded in a register (A1 to A7).

{/* DRAFT */}
The results feed a scroll essay built around one continuous line that runs through every chart. A "Draw the Line" interaction asks readers to guess the relationship between acclaim and profit before showing it.

## How it works

{/* DRAFT */}
Each hypothesis is falsifiable and tested in statsmodels. A 13-run sensitivity framework varies the modelled assumptions, and the output is frozen into one JSON contract the site reads.

## What it found

{/* DRAFT */}
Returns are heavily skewed. $1.62bn deployed for a net profit of −$37m. Mean ROI is +191% but the median is −10%, only 43% of films were profitable, and five films made about 65% of all the profit. Skew is 4.62 and the Gini coefficient 0.63.

{/* DRAFT */}
Acclaim does pay. The Metascore coefficient is +0.047 (robust standard errors 0.018, p = 0.008): ten Metascore points are worth about +60% on the revenue multiple. The Spearman correlation is 0.32. The result held in all 13 sensitivity runs.

## Where it breaks

{/* DRAFT */}
P&A spend and rental rates are modelled, not reported, so every profit figure carries the assumptions in the register. The core sample is 58 films.
