## The question

{/* DRAFT */}
How did the largest IPO in history break the rules? SpaceX listed in June 2026 at $1.77tn. I set out to explain the mechanics, the valuation and what happened after listing.

## What I built

{/* DRAFT */}
A scroll-driven analysis in chapters: the mechanics of the deal, five deviations from IPO convention, valuation, listing day and the aftermath. One chapter sets out my own fair-value position, a Watch panel holds dated predictions, and a method register shows where sources disagree.

## How it works

{/* DRAFT */}
A Python notebook (yfinance) produces the supply, money-left-on-the-table, drawdown and implied-growth analyses, with comparables and IPO records tables. Every figure on the page is bound from JSON with a source and an as-of date.

{/* DRAFT */}
The site is Vite and plain JavaScript with GSAP, hand-built SVG charts and a raw WebGL shader. No frameworks or chart libraries, so I can explain every line.

## What it found

{/* DRAFT */}
A $135 IPO price and a 94× multiple. The five deviations from convention, and my fair-value view, are in the essay.

## Where it breaks

{/* DRAFT */}
The notebook still runs on stub price data, labelled as such on screen, until it is rerun on live data. Some historical raise figures and live comparable multiples are still to be verified.
